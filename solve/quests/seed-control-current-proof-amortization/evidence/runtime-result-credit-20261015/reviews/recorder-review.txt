R4 [scope/semantics, qualification]: The parent's amended ordinary-function
proposal can preserve the exact original Promise/return value and synchronous
throw, unlike an async wrapper. For this existing native-Promise contract,
attach Promise.resolve(answer).then(observe, rejectionNoOp), then return answer.
The fulfillment observer must catch record/clone/accessor failures into a
fixture-owned error list. That list is asserted separately after owned cleanup;
do not throw observation failures into the caller's delivery path. Underlying

This is not a universal JavaScript transparency claim: assimilating an arbitrary
thenable can invoke its then again when a caller also awaits the original;
attaching a rejection handler can affect unhandled-rejection diagnostics.
The actual seam is explicitly async (L:96) and its callers await delivery.
Do not expand the factory's advertised contract to hostile thenables, altered
Promise intrinsics or synchronous observation timing. No production transport
change is needed or authorized for this narrow repair.

R5 [ordering/evidence, qualification]: The amended observer records fulfillment
after completion, before its caller's continuation on the same ordinary native

Nested wrappers register in order and do not await each other's observation.
Moving RESUME capture after fulfillment is acceptable for this fixture because
N:96 awaits the initial release and N:105-115 consumes captured requests only
later. A fulfilled delivery may still contain a refusal; capture is not semantic
approval. Keep original PAUSE assertions. Post-completion clones describe that
observation time, not immutable submitted wire bytes or durable authorization.
