## Permission boundary

The permission-changing release decision is **the committed control-row transition to RESUME_AUTHORIZED**, not submission, a pin response, an HTTP page, origin installation or transport ACK. Dependent RESUME and exact local admission projection remain separate consumers of the complete decision and applicable current identities (D:1739–1745,1777–1805,2231–2279). A complete persisted authorization may survive restart; partial observations from a superseded incarnation may not manufacture that complete decision.

**Registration is a new write.** Actual `src/bootstrap/bootstrap-service-seed-workflow.js:345–362` acquires the early SQL runtime/handler and calls release before the registration phase. `src/bootstrap/phases/seed-registration-phase.js:107–128,186–190` then enables its writer and performs real registration/upsert work. D:368–373,2431–2434 retains full applicable authorization before this work. Snapshot/discovery, original CDC/ACK and already accepted transaction recovery keep their own closed-admission permissions; naming a registration call “recovery” supplies none.

## All remaining findings

### R7-1 — HIGH — submission remains an unsafe cutoff for invalidating partial authority

The amendment fixes two real parts of R6-1: D:2192–2206 separates control-member claims from dependent-target receipts, and :2208–2219 explicitly extends controlled lifecycle actuation to obtain retirement/readback before replacing the old identity. Those are coherent owner expansions.

The remaining gap is **after final command submission but before its committed decision**. D:2240–2244 and :2259–2265 only prevent a *new submission* after observed invalidation. If the final command is already in flight, it allows final authorization to win the control-row race against a later retirement. The final command still consumes the captured row/claim revisions; invalidating the volatile pin does not change those durable predicates. The text therefore does not enforce its claim that current pins are required at finalization.

Counterexample under the specified protocol:

1. A1 and the other control members have partial shares and return exact pins. The control row is RESERVED; no complete authorization exists.
2. The release owner submits the last-share command. Hold that command before durable application.
3. The actual owner observes A1's connection/lifecycle invalidation, closes admission and invalidates the pin as D:2221–2229 requires. It submits retirement, but that application is delayed. This is a **known** invalidation, not an undetected crash.
4. Deliver the older final command first. Its captured CURRENT claims/revisions still match, so :2261–2265 allows RESUME_AUTHORIZED to be manufactured from the now-invalid A1 observation.

The same schedule applies to target confirmation: withhold a submitted confirmation, observe and close an exact target, delay its receipt retirement, then apply the old confirmation. D:2275–2276 requires valid target pins but supplies no committed enforcement of their intervening invalidation. A local successor refusing RESUME protects that successor; it does not repair an invalid complete authorization or prevent clearing the only unresolved confirmation debt.

Actual producer boundaries matter: `src/transport/message-router-connection-close-reconnect.js:103–137` can synchronously identify a matching close and then fails pending requests/emits its event; `src/partition/partition-participant-transaction-pause.js:33–51` independently invalidates local admission when native term/leader/configuration/runtime/lifecycle changes. Expanding those producers as proposed can stop future submissions, but cannot retract a command already submitted through the consensus owner. Putting retirement in a direct call instead of the queue does not establish ordering before that pending command.

The same unresolved-outcome edge needs a single lifetime rule: :2245 retains pins through applied result/readback, while :2251–2256 expires/discards them at deadline/restart. An expired or restarted invocation must not leave an old submitted command able to grant fresh authorization from vanished pins. A new process promising to repin before its *next* submission does not fence the prior one.

**Required correction:** specify the durable finalization protocol/owner ordering for an exact observed invalidation while a submitted decision remains unresolved. The committed permission boundary must consume admissible evidence; submission must not silently become an irrevocable partial grant. Define cancellation/fencing or the applicable authority transition and lost-answer recovery without making replicated apply depend on arbitrary volatile per-replica observations. If the proposed semantics cannot satisfy that user boundary, surface the incompatibility rather than label an uncommitted submission complete. Preserve deterministic application and all native consensus traffic.

Extend D:2281–2289's witnesses with **submit → latch before commit → known invalidation/expiry or restart → delayed final command → delayed retirement**, for both authorization and confirmation. The existing inverse positive must instead establish **complete committed authorization/confirmation before the later change**, then show exact durable recovery. Its current “submits the final command first” wording is insufficient. Pair with fresh current-incarnation recovery and the unchanged startup positive. This does not demand instantaneous knowledge of an unobserved remote failure or revoke an already complete decision.

### R7-2 — HIGH — new route-page limits stop after materialization and do not own concurrent send retention

R6-3's main architectural correction is good: D:2350–2357 removes full system/debt snapshots; :2428–2442 leaves large obligations with their existing recovery owners. The recipient now has a bounded Content-Length/incremental-body/JSON path (:2402–2414), with separate cell/page/overlay limits. Numeric default measurements can remain future source-proof work.

Two resource edges remain unspecified in this **new producer/transfer**, independently of choosing those defaults:

1. **Before source allocation.** D:2378–2385 resolves/reads one full routing cell, then checks whether it fits and rejects an oversized cell. The page limit does not bound that first cell's row/string allocation. Neither the minimum PARTITIONS/SERVICES/endpoint projection nor each variable field is tied to a pre-read size bound. “Own primitive fields once” and UTF-8 counting after the read are useful validation, but do not prevent an unsupported large cell from being materialized. The global witness-digest pass at :2371–2375 also needs an explicitly bounded streaming traversal; it must not retain a full census or allocate an unbounded cursor before producing the bounded page. Existing `src/query/query-result-budget.js:62–89` can use `.all()` without limits, or iterate a materialized row and stringify it to measure; it does not supply the missing preallocation guarantee.
2. **From request admission through actual network release.** “One request produces one page” and “one seed invocation consumes one page” (:2393–2394) bound neither concurrent incoming producers nor old response buffers still queued while another attempt begins. The listed configuration fields (:2391–2393) contain byte/row limits but no consumed aggregate producer claim. The statement that simultaneous copy limits are separate configuration (:2385–2389) does not say which existing owner acquires the charge, before which read, or when the sender finally releases it. The actual `BootstrapRequestOwnerHandler` acquires request admission at `src/bootstrap/owners/bootstrap-request-owner-handler.js:335–344` and releases it in `finally` at :667–669, after returning the response object (:611), before that returned payload's network lifetime is necessarily over. The new read-only purpose must explicitly consume a suitable existing capacity owner without requiring join readiness, and transfer/retain its byte claim until HTTP finish/close has released the payload. Reusing the existing request count unchanged is not a retained-byte bound.

A delayed receiver plus repeated/overlapping startup contacts can therefore retain multiple encoded pages after the work-count claims end; an individually oversized route row can allocate before its proposed UNSUPPORTED refusal. These are the prior allocation/lifetime counterexamples at the newly narrowed page seam, not a demand for a whole-server RSS bound or a new generic resource framework.

**Required correction:** name the bounded projected field/read contract or preflight that caps a source cell before variable materialization; bound the all-witness/cursor traversal; bind the new recovery-purpose producer to existing shared capacity before reading/encoding; carry that claim through the pre-encoded buffer and actual HTTP release. Specify overflow, cancel, disconnect, late completion and retry ownership so a deadline only ends a caller wait and cannot free bytes still held by the sender/consumer. Bounds can be parameterized now and measured before source approval; the required owner/claim/lifetime transitions cannot be deferred to measurement.

Keep D:2436–2442's >16 MiB backlog positive, and add the paired oversized-source-cell pre-read refusal plus blocked HTTP send/overlapping contact schedule. Demonstrate bounded admitted copies and later supported completion without losing debt, resetting deadlines or turning partial routes into a complete population. Cache hydration, event-obligation delivery and a single unsupported row remain distinct proof scopes.
