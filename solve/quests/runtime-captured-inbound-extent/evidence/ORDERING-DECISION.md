## Selected replacement guarantee, proposed for review

1. **One finite temporal cut.** After successful execution/reconstruction admission, capture generation and the whole pending extent once. All preaccepted occurrences, including a refused message's valid tail, precede O under the existing failure rules. Arrivals after C retain authoritative queue custody and owned later service. Their presence cannot repeatedly reset this turn's remaining extent.
2. **Native authority at effect.** O reads and acts on the core's actual state through the existing operation owner, with existing lifecycle, persistence and admission checks. No envelope-header inspection grants or revokes authority. A post-capture arrival is concurrent input that may be ordered after O; the receipt records what O actually did, not what processing that arrival first would have done.
3. **Acceptance is historical, completion is separate.** Transfer acceptance does not prove present leadership, completed handoff or permission to remove a replica. Proposal acceptance does not prove commit/application. Once O may have produced an effect, later role changes or response uncertainty cannot justify a fresh request identity, blind replay or a fabricated no-effect refusal. Existing result/custody owners retain the obligation.
4. **Preserved checks and progress.** Pre-capture processed-first equivalence, handled refusal/tail ordering, current-generation checks before dequeue, Ready durability/application ordering and owned remainder wakes remain required. Each captured occurrence completes or yields its existing typed failure; later ingress does not increase the extent. This bounds the number of captured occurrences, not elapsed latency, an arbitrary initial backlog or service under permanent storage/quorum failure. No second drain, INSPECT loop, term whitelist or per-operation scheduler is selected.

The proposed supersession is limited to F1's expectation that post-capture arrivals during an awaited send must precede this selected decision. Historical F1 records and all original test bytes remain preserved. Its other observations are converted only by a reviewed, explicit replacement guarantee, never silently waived because 152 cases passed.

## Consumer blocker and smallest same-schedule proof

The direct production `.propose()` census has three callers: partition write commit, message-group routing, and Wasm service. The Wasm owner must settle the discrepancy between its documented/default strong-write behavior and acceptance-only implementation. `TimerManager` additionally awaits proposal acceptance before local timer changes/callback invocation, while committed timer state has a separate application path. The timer callback risk is conditional component behavior: the source census found no production callback installer or create/cancel caller, so live callback reachability is not established. These are existing owner obligations; do not repair them with extra runtime draining. The message-group legacy path also applies cache before proposing and acknowledges routing without an established committed-delivery receipt; the existence of `buildCommittedCdcOutcome` (no production caller found) does not close that custody proof. These remain the existing message-group/CDC and Wasm owners’ obligations. No blanket consumer-safe supersession is approved while these gaps are open.
