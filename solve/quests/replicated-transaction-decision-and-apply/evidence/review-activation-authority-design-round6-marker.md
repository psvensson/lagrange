## Permission boundary and registration ordering

The proposed release permission change is the **committed final-share transition of the control generation row from RESERVED to RESUME_AUTHORIZED**, consuming the complete fixed control population (D:1739–1745,1924–1938). A dependent committed RESUME records deterministic target authorization; the exact target's local admission projection then consumes that complete authorization together with current local identity/membership/pause facts (D:1777–1805,2019–2048). A complete admitted decision can survive restart. Partial observations cannot become a complete decision after their applicable incarnation has already been superseded.

First-origin authorization is separately owned: the startup/command record's effect CAS authorizes an exact ENABLE/origin effect (D:911–934), or the local durable CREATE/physical claim does so. Neither an origin nor a topology label is, alone, permission to open current mutation admission.

**Seed registration is a new write.** The design correctly places activation at REGISTRATION_READY before those writes (D:368–373,1628–1629,2136–2142). Actual `src/bootstrap/phases/seed-registration-phase.js:107–128` enables the writer and performs registration; `registerMessageGroup` performs an upsert at :186–190. Current `bootstrap-service-seed-workflow.js:345–362` calls release before running that phase. This review grants no permission to move registration into an internal-recovery exception. It must follow complete applicable authorization, or a separately valid existing bootstrap authorization consumed by its real owner. Read-only discovery, original CDC replay/ACK, snapshot recovery and already accepted transaction settlement have their own permissions while ordinary writes remain closed.

## Complete findings

### R6-1 — HIGH — queued retirement cannot guarantee HELD before final authorization/confirmation

D:1912–1922 adds conditional claim publish/retire commands, then relies on tracked-service/consensus/socket events to **enqueue** retirement. The stated guarantee that a “missed or unavailable retirement leaves final authorization HELD” is not implemented by the specified last-share predicate at :1924–1928: it checks the existing row's CURRENT claim and matching share revision. Until the retirement actually commits, that predicate still succeeds. Preventing a successor from becoming *release-eligible* does not invalidate the old partial share used to authorize another dependent.

The actual cited producers establish a concrete ordering gap:

- `src/node/replica-handler-membership-relay.js:44–58` updates/deletes the tracked service **before** notifying listeners. Its owner comment says this relay carries no authority.
- `src/partition/partition-participant-transaction-connection.js:30–33` stamps a newly opened connection synchronously; it has no committed retirement handshake.
- `src/node/replica-handler-release-recovery.js:121–130` turns service/consensus changes into queued work. `enqueue`/`dispatch` at :48–55,97–118 do not serialize that event with a remote final-share application.
- `src/transport/message-router-connection-close-reconnect.js:126–137` clears connection fields, then emits CONNECTION_CLOSED containing only `nodeId`. It does not supply the exact replica/claim/connection generation to retire. Calling this an authenticated event does not fill in that binding.

Reachable proposed schedule: A1's share is committed; its actual lifecycle/connection owner changes or retires A1 and queues the required retirement; that retirement is delayed/unavailable; B's final-share command arrives first. The stored A1 claim still says CURRENT and its share revision matches, so D:1924's command authorizes despite the locally superseded partial observation. The required witness at :1940–1945 instead waits for retirement and A2 publication before releasing B; that only proves the already-committed-retirement case.

The same gap remains for **confirmation**. D:2034–2044 says a term/leader/connection change closes local admission and causes receipt retirement. While that retirement is queued, a historical CURRENT OPENED set can still confirm and clear the slot. `partition-participant-transaction-pause.js:33–51` closes local admission synchronously on changed native facts, independently of that later row command. Also bind the proposed `target claim revision` (:2035) to its actual producer: :1897–1909 defines claims for fixed **control** members, whereas OPENED receipts concern the **dependent** targets. These identity domains must not be silently interchanged.

**Required correction:** define the exact owner ordering/handshake that makes an observed or controlled incarnation/receipt invalidation participate in the same final-decision serialization, before a stale partial share or receipt can be consumed. Name the conditional invalidation result, exact old claim/target identity, successor publication gate and lost-answer/readback behavior. A row marked CURRENT cannot be treated as fresh merely because its invalidation RPC has not committed. Extend the actual producer boundary explicitly where needed, rather than relying on an eventual wake.

This does **not** demand instantaneous knowledge of an undetected remote crash or continuous distributed freshness after a completed decision. State the authoritative supersession point and what is unknown before it. The required counterexample is a change already performed/observed by the named owner, with its retirement command deliberately withheld. Pair it with current-claim recovery and the inverse ordering where complete authorization commits first and remains owed after later retirement. For receipts, latch the local close before the final confirmation and prove it cannot consume the obsolete OPENED fact. Preserve native fencing and deterministic apply; do not redefine partial votes as irrevocable complete authorization.

### R6-2 — HIGH — the durable-seed cold-contact input/response handoff is still incomplete

D:2070–2076 says the cold existing-origin branch obtains candidates from durable peer addresses “already collected by resolveAutoRejoinStartupDecision.” They are collected internally, but its supported **durable-seed result discards them**:

- `src/bootstrap/rejoin-hints.js:354–369` collects hints and durable NODES addresses; :534–545 delegates durable seed to its seed-serving decision owner; :645–648 returns only the built decision.
- `src/bootstrap/seed-recovery-proof.js:105–127` returns a valid durable-seed decision with `peerAddress:null`, **`peerAddresses:[]`** and unavailable peer-address state, even when peer evidence satisfied recovery.
- `src/entrypoint-runtime-join-decision.js:446–453` again returns `seedNodeAddress:null` and **`seedNodeAddresses:[]`** on the seed branch.
- `src/bootstrap/phases/contact-seed-phase.js:97–101` requires nonempty candidates. Its current constructor/delegates are attached to the join owner; no retained-seed candidate handoff is described by D beyond the assumption above.

Thus a valid retained seed with stored peer hints and remote control/system leaders can reach the proposed new contact path with an empty candidate list. Classifying that as “no candidate → HOLD/operator recovery” would lose an available normal recovery path, rather than faithfully reporting absent evidence.

The return side also needs its explicit consumer expansion. D:2085–2091 defines `kind`, `clusterId`, `responderNodeId`, snapshots/witnesses/debt; current `ContactSeedPhase.executeSeedContactAttempt` at :263–280 requires `response.success`, saves the ordinary `bootstrapResponse`, and derives `seedNodeId`/`seedNodeWsAddress`. `startup-sql-runtime-handoff.js:112–123` consumes `systemTableSnapshots` only; it does not validate or carry the proposed per-partition witnesses. D explicitly proposes new server/witness behavior and leadership-gate consumption, but its statement that the response is consumed “exactly as the join path already consumes bootstrapResponse” (:2096–2098) does not establish the typed recovery-purpose response path. Do not send a read-only recovery answer through the join admission/assignment semantics by accident.

**Required correction:** expand the existing membership/seed-serving/entrypoint owner handoff to retain a separate typed **route-hint** candidate set on the durable-seed path, without changing its membership/freshness verdict. Name the seed-side ContactSeedPhase composition and its validated recovery-purpose response consumer, including success/refusal shapes, responder identity/address and per-partition witness handoff. Preserve candidate rotation/redirect handling, but explicitly clamp its context/each HTTP attempt to the inherited startup invocation: the current `createSeedContactContext` at :153–172 initializes its own `startTime:now()` and retry timeout, so merely reentering that phase is not an inherited deadline.

Pair a normal existing-origin **seed-mode** restart with real stored peer hints, no explicit CLI seed override and no seeded cache against stale/partial/no-candidate responses. The first must acquire the real HTTP recovery envelope and progress through the same owner; the latter must HOLD without manufacturing freshness or admitting registration writes. The same future candidate must also pass the unchanged normal pgwire startup path. This is an explicit owner-contract omission, not a demand that the proposed new code already exist.

### R6-3 — HIGH — the new complete cold-recovery envelope has no supported allocation/continuation contract

D:2100–2121 introduces a new producer: complete **unpaged SELECTs** for every required system partition, accumulated into one HTTP envelope including route/system rows and unfinished release/CDC obligations. D:2136–2143 makes receiving this envelope a prerequisite to the original replay and activation that enable registration. Neither these clauses nor the release-message limits at :1974–1999 specify a supported size, per-partition/aggregate admission, retained response lifetime, or bounded continuation for this new envelope. The 1024-member/1 MiB release aggregate is a different resource; it does not bound the unfinished rows or the bootstrap response.

The existing named owners do not supply that missing guarantee automatically:

- `src/bootstrap/owners/bootstrap-topology-snapshot-owner-published-views.js:159–175` currently builds an envelope from cache-backed rows; replacing its producer with full authoritative reads is an explicit new responsibility.
- `bootstrap-request-owner-handler.js:518–558,590–611` assembles the complete ordinary bootstrap response and checks remaining time before returning it. A remaining deadline is not row/byte admission before envelope materialization or serialization.
- The actual reused HTTP client, `src/bootstrap/node-joining-backfill-merge-and-status.js:336–394`, bounds time but consumes the whole success body with `response.json()` at :392; the error branch also materializes `response.text()` and a parsed copy at :365–375. It has no response-byte or continuation contract in the proposed call.
- The existing query collector `src/query/query-result-budget.js:62–89` takes optional limits, can use `.all()` without them, and otherwise measures each already-produced row with `JSON.stringify`. Those general query facilities cannot establish this new producer's complete-envelope bound, nor guarantee progress when one required result exceeds a configured query result cap. This review does not change or independently reject that general subsystem.

A valid large unfinished backlog has two unspecified outcomes: retaining/materializing an oversized full response, or repeatedly receiving a bounded-query refusal while cold startup cannot reach the recovery owner that drains it. A capped query that correctly refuses does not make the full startup requirement complete. The proposal's required nonempty debt positive does not exercise this case, and the accepted preflight resource witness only covers release messages.

**Required correction:** define this cold-response owner's supported row and aggregate domain, admission before its variable read/envelope/encoding allocations, bounded in-flight/copy retention through actual HTTP consumption, and what the recipient does on typed resource refusal. Provide bounded continuation or a minimal authoritative bootstrap handoff that reaches the existing bounded debt-recovery owner without first materializing the whole backlog; continuation must preserve complete per-partition evidence and cannot turn a partial range into absence/completion. Retain the same caller invocation, cancellation and eventual consumer-release rules. Name the existing owners being expanded; no new ledger, raw SQL path, limit raise or whole-server RSS framework is requested.

Pair normal cold remote startup with actual supported backlog above 16 MiB and bounded progress, plus a withheld/pressure/oversized-input refusal that preserves debt and later resumes. Distinguish a backlog of supported rows from a single unsupported oversized row and from C17 cache-state hydration: none automatically certifies the other. This finding is confined to the newly proposed bootstrap recovery producer/consumer and its required startup dependency, not a reopened C17 or general query review.
