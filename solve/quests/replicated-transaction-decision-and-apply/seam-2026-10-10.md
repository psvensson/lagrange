# TX1 seam record - coordinator / participant / replication (2026-10-10)

Risk IDs: TX1 (P0), with TX2 following. Source of the recommendation:
[adversarial risk review](../../../docs/development/adversarial-risk-review-2026-10-10.md)
and [local priorities](../../epics/raft-rs-full-cutover/local-priorities-2026-10-10.md).
Design of record: PR100 Leg A at `daddead73ffeb8c348663695ac2f0eaa8873cf75`
(`solve/changes/0-3-prepared-transactions-split-merge/design.md`, design only).
This record names the seam; it approves no coordinator change and is not a
contract supersession.

## Lanes

| Lane | Owner surface | Owns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Query (separate cloud lane; owner not present in this session) | `src/query/distributed/distributed-transaction-protocol.js`, `distributed-transaction-recovery.js`, `DistributedTransactionCoordinator` | frozen participant set, one-/two-phase selection, the durable coordinator decision record, fanout, recovery sweep, participant outcome reads |
| Local (this lane) | `src/partition/partition-service-transaction-base.js`, `partition-service-write-metrics-base.js`, `partition-service-entry-apply-base.js`, the rs-raft runtime/durable store, split/merge topology owners | PREPARE/COMMIT/ROLLBACK as committed partition commands, atomic apply of operations + outcome + applied index, exact participant outcome answers, CDC after commit, later the admission/drain fence (TX2) |

The seam is the participant's committed-command vocabulary and its outcome
read, consumed by the coordinator:

1. `PREPARE_TRANSACTION` becomes a committed, applied partition command carrying
   normalized session/transaction identity, transaction epoch, deterministic
   staged operations (the `{type, table, sql, params}` entries the write path
   already admits), the write set digest and a prepared command identity. The
   participant acknowledges PREPARE only after its group committed and applied
   the command. `LOCAL_STAGING` stops being an advertised prepared state.
2. `TRANSACTION_COMMIT` / `TRANSACTION_ROLLBACK` are decisions applied by the
   committed-entry owner: COMMIT executes the prepared operations exactly once in
   the same application transaction as the terminal outcome and the applied
   index; ROLLBACK terminalizes without applying. Replays are idempotent;
   conflicting content or outcome reversal is refused.
3. The participant answers an exact outcome read (`resolveParticipantCommitOutcome`
   consumer side) from durable state: COMMITTED, NOT_COMMITTED (ROLLED_BACK) or
   UNKNOWN. `NO_TRANSACTION` or a missing in-memory session is never an answer.
4. The coordinator persists one immutable, monotonic decision before COMMIT
   fanout and never selects rollback after a COMMIT decision; timeout, lost
   responses or a missing process-local session delay completion only. This is
   the query lane's change; it stays outstanding here.

## Consumer falsifier recorded for the query owner

`resolveParticipantCommitMiss` in `distributed-transaction-protocol.js`
returns `COMMIT_MISS_RESOLUTION.COMMITTED` for a two-phase transaction on
`NO_TRANSACTION` without any outcome read (lines around 669-685 on this
candidate). Falsifier: drive the exported method with `commitMode = 2PC`, a
COMMITTING stage, a `NO_TRANSACTION` error and an injected
`resolveParticipantCommitOutcome` that answers UNKNOWN and counts calls; the
required result is UNKNOWN (or the exact durable outcome) with one outcome read,
never COMMITTED with zero reads. The same file's `abortTimedOutTransaction`
must be unreachable once a durable COMMIT decision exists. These are recorded,
not implemented, in this lane.

## Decisions the query owner must take (revision 2, 2026-10-10)

1. Transaction identity: the coordinator allocates one non-reused `transactionId`
   (`sql_transactions.transaction_id` already exists) before any fanout and
   carries it, with `participantId`, `commitMode` and `transactionEpoch`, on
   every BEGIN/write/PREPARE/COMMIT/ROLLBACK/outcome request; the participant
   refuses requests without it (`TRANSACTION_ID_REQUIRED`).
2. One immutable decision: a conditional monotonic write on `sql_transactions`
   before COMMIT fanout; uncertain decision writes resolved before an incompatible
   decision; participant UNKNOWN is pending, never COMMITTED; no rollback after
   the decision. Falsifiers: the `resolveParticipantCommitMiss` NO_TRANSACTION case
   above, and `abortTimedOutTransaction` reachable after a COMMIT decision.
3. 1PC: choose prepare-first (A) or participant-decided intent/commit (B) as in
   `design-leg-a-v2-2026-10-10.md` section 7; (B) requires explicit supersession
   of the sealed "decision before fanout" promise for 1PC. The participant
   implements (A) until (B) is chosen.
4. Outcome semantics: the participant answers UNKNOWN for an absent row; the
   coordinator's durable decision record is the authority for "never prepared".
5. Register the coordinator-participant coupled pair with its discriminating
   witness in `test/shards/impact-contracts.json` when the seam is implemented.

The query owner was not present in this session; these items are recorded, not
agreed. Participant implementation of items that do not depend on them (the
reservation, durable PREPARED rows, terminal authority, isolation) may proceed
after the revised design is independently accepted.

### Landing rule (supersedes the last paragraph of the revision-2 section)

The revision-2 section ends with "Participant implementation of items that do
not depend on them ... may proceed after the revised design is independently
accepted". That is superseded: there is one protocol and one cutover change
set spanning coordinator and participant, landed together under this seam's
agreement. No legacy acceptance path survives in any landed head and no landed
head breaks current transactions, so **no participant source lands before the
query owner agrees these items**. Until then the participant lane delivers only
the design, the witnesses and the supersession inventory. Revision-2 item 3's
"the participant implements (A) until (B) is chosen" is superseded the same
way: nothing is implemented before the 1PC choice (item F).

### A. Request and answer contract (one canonical shape, R08)

Every BEGIN / session write / PREPARE / COMMIT / ROLLBACK / outcome request to
a participant carries `transactionId`, `participantId`, `commitMode`,
`transactionEpoch` (and `sessionId` as routing context). COMMIT and bound
ROLLBACK add `decision`, `preparedDigest`, `decisionText`, `decisionDigest`.
Session reads and writes are QUERY requests carrying the same identity (today
only `sessionId` is added, `src/query/query-executor-partition-request-builders.js:80`).
Answers carry `transactionId`, `participantId`, `state`, `outcome` and, where
relevant, `failureCode`, `deferRetry`, `preparedDigest`, `prepareIndex`,
`prepareTerm`, `decisionIndex`, `decisionTerm`, `refusalCause`, `results`,
`provisional` (design section 2.2). A request missing identity is refused
`participant_transaction_identity_required`.

Engine changes: the four participant callbacks and the outcome callback
(`src/query/sql-query-engine-instance-initializer.js:190-231`) and
`deliverTransactionOperation` (`src/query/sql-query-engine.js:569-627`) carry
the identity; the delivery identity becomes `{transactionId, partitionId,
operation}` instead of `{sessionId, partitionId, operation}`
(`src/query/sql-query-engine.js:66-96`).

### B. Identity

`transactionId = nodeId/bootIncarnation/sequence`. The coordinator receives
`nodeId` and the runtime's boot incarnation at construction (held at
`src/lagrange-runtime-startup.js:107`; owner `reserveBootIncarnation`,
`src/bootstrap/boot-incarnation-owner.js:196`, durable and never reissued,
contract `:13-24`). `createTransactionId`
(`src/query/distributed/distributed-transaction-records.js:79-82`, today
`tx-${sessionId}-${now()}-${seq}`) uses the existing in-process sequence
(`distributed-transaction-coordinator.js:115`), no clock. The id is persisted by
`registerWorkflow` in `begin` (`distributed-transaction-coordinator.js:261`)
before the first participant BEGIN (`:331`). Falsifier W10a (red: two
coordinators mint `tx-default-1000-1`).

### C. Immutable decision record and bound fanout

- A new insert-once system table, proposed `sql_transaction_decisions
  (transaction_id PRIMARY KEY, decision, decision_text, decision_digest,
  created_at)`, beside `sql_transactions` in
  `src/bootstrap/system-table-workflow-schema-definitions.js`, written with the
  gateway's INSERT kind (`src/control-plane/control-plane-system-table-gateway-constants.js:153-158`).
  A separate table keeps the decision out of reach of the status UPSERT
  (`src/query/sql-query-engine.js:114-147`) whatever that UPSERT's column
  semantics are. First insert wins; a duplicate key is the deterministic
  constraint failure, after which the coordinator reads the row and follows it.
- `decision_text = JSON.stringify({transactionId, decision, participants:
  [[participantId, preparedDigest|null], ...sorted]})`, `decision_digest =
  sha256(decision_text)`; COMMIT requires every participant's PREPARED digest
  (item E). Participants verify the text, their own entry and the digest.
- The decision is inserted before any COMMIT or ROLLBACK fanout. An insert
  whose outcome is unknown is resolved (re-delivered under its entryId, or the
  row read) before any incompatible decision; it is never assumed either way.
  The persistence skip without a gateway (`sql-query-engine.js:115-117`) must
  not apply to the decision: unavailability is a typed failure (R11).
- Every rollback path inserts the ROLLBACK decision first and carries its
  binding: `abortTimedOutTransaction`
  (`src/query/distributed/distributed-transaction-protocol.js:258-275`) and its
  call sites (`:296`, `:313`, `:324`, `:344`, `:351`, `:366`, `:392`), the
  prepare-failure rollback (`:322-342`), and recovery's rollback path
  (`distributed-transaction-recovery.js:310-313`, `:429-431`). If the insert
  finds COMMIT, the coordinator continues commit (recovery with
  `allowTimedOutCommitStatuses`), never rollback.
- Participant defence (independent of the coordinator change): an unbound
  ROLLBACK against a PREPARED row is refused
  `participant_transaction_decision_binding_required` (W9).
- Falsifier S1 (executable, red): a 2PC transaction whose second participant's
  COMMIT answer is lost past the budget today rolls back `['p1', 'p2']` after
  COMMITTING; required: no rollback call, status stays COMMITTING.
- An unbound ROLLBACK may be sent only to a participant whose PREPARE was never
  sent (it only discards a volatile session and answers `durable: false`).

### D. Outcome semantics

`resolveParticipantCommitOutcome(transactionId, participantId)` answers from the
participant's durable row: COMMITTED; NOT_COMMITTED only from a ROLLED_BACK or
REFUSED row; UNKNOWN for PREPARED or absent. UNKNOWN is pending, never COMMITTED
and never NOT_COMMITTED. `resolveParticipantCommitMiss`
(`distributed-transaction-protocol.js:669-689`) must read the outcome in both
modes (today `:673-675` returns COMMITTED for 2PC with zero reads). Falsifier S2
(executable, red). A COMMIT reaching a COMMITTED row is answered COMMITTED, so
the NO_TRANSACTION commit miss no longer arises from the participant.

### E. PREPARE answers retained

A PREPARED answer carries `preparedDigest`, `prepareIndex`, `prepareTerm`. The
coordinator persists them on its participant row
(`sql_transaction_participants`, schema `system-table-workflow-schema-definitions.js:39-64`) before inserting a COMMIT
decision. A PREPARE answered UNKNOWN (deadline, leader loss) is retried under
the same identity; if it never resolves to PREPARED, the coordinator decides
ROLLBACK (bound), which the participant applies as ROLLED_BACK or a tombstone
in log order. Recovery of a PREPARED transaction whose digests were not
persisted re-reads them with outcome reads (PREPARED rows return the digest)
or decides ROLLBACK.

### F. 1PC

Choose prepare-first (recommended: no participant 1PC semantics; supersedes
`test/query/distributed-transaction-coordinator.test.js:646-673` "one phase must
not prepare" and `architecture/images-distributed-public-seam.md:102`; delete
`COMMIT_MODE.ONE_PHASE_COMMIT` rather than relabel it) or participant-decided
(one round; needs a quest supersession of "decision before fanout" for 1PC,
owner decision). Design section 11.2.

### G. Recovery ownership and the reservation bound

The participant never re-enters the coordinator. A lost coordinator's decision
is reached by any SQL engine's recovery: every engine loads every
`sql_transactions` row (`sql-query-engine-transaction-recovery-methods.js:151-159`)
and sweeps every 1000 ms (`distributed-transaction-coordinator-constants.js:46`)
over transactions past their 60 s budget (`distributed-transaction-recovery.js:404-431`).
Bound on a PREPARED reservation: budget + one sweep + one participant round
while an engine runs recovery; otherwise unbounded (typed write refusals on
that partition, R12 exposure). Several engines may drive one transaction
concurrently (`runExclusive` is per process); the insert-once decision is what
makes that safe (finding F-REC).
