# Diagnose post-spread `replica_operations-p1` write timeouts

Quest: `formation-ledger-self-move-blocks-cluster-ops`  
Artifact: `data/examples/service-data-affinity-demo/node-{0..4}.log` (2026-07-06 cold 5-node formation)

## Bottom line

The binding post-spread head is **not** that the emergency ADD created `replica_operations-p1-r4` on node-4. The logs show `r4` was created by the first ledger `REPLACE` on **node-1** (`1ee9936a...`), became the ledger leader, and stayed the canonical write target; the emergency ADD on node-4 (`ef566759...`) created **`r5`**, not `r4` (`node-1.log:211`-`node-1.log:214`, `node-1.log:273`-`node-1.log:290`, `node-4.log:279`-`node-4.log:305`). After the successful spread, `replica_operations-p1` remained **over target** (4 active replicas for target 3), and cleanup/self-move attempts were skipped by `operation_ledger_self_move_waiting_for_idle_ledger` instead of draining the surplus (`node-1.log:610`-`node-1.log:614`, `node-1.log:1084`-`node-1.log:1088`). The sustained row-write failures route to `r4`; `r4` is alive and applying some writes/CDC, but it is also a leader with a stuck transaction/durability-fitness problem, so routed writes frequently do not produce a service response before the pending-response timeout (`node-0.log:3704`-`node-0.log:3705`, `node-1.log:763`-`node-1.log:798`, `node-1.log:2770`, `node-1.log:4256`-`node-1.log:4257`).

## D2. Why `r4` times out

### What the timeout means

`Pending response timeout` is not a raw “could not connect” error. The message-router pending-response ledger arms the handler-response timeout **after ACK** and rejects the waiter when no `SERVICE_RESPONSE` arrives in time (`src/transport/message-router-pending-response-ledger.js:270`-`src/transport/message-router-pending-response-ledger.js:291`). When a response arrives after its waiter was retired, the router logs `No pending service response request`; the inbound-dispatch code explicitly says a non-error response after retirement “proves the peer received and handled the request” (`src/transport/message-router-inbound-dispatch.js:345`-`src/transport/message-router-inbound-dispatch.js:368`). The live log has exactly that late/orphaned-response signal on node-1 at `15:29:21.746Z`, with `suppressedSinceLastWarn:7241` (`node-1.log:5365`). That line is not message-id-correlated to a specific failed mutation, so it is supporting evidence, not a per-write proof.

### The failed participant is the canonical leader candidate, not a replica fanout list

Operation row persistence goes through the control-plane system-table gateway: `persistNewOperationUnlocked()` submits an INSERT to `replica_operations`, and `persistOperationUpdate()` submits UPDATEs (`src/rebalancer/replica-operation-repository-mutation-persistence-methods.js:51`-`src/rebalancer/replica-operation-repository-mutation-persistence-methods.js:90`, `src/rebalancer/replica-operation-repository-mutation-persistence-methods.js:151`-`src/rebalancer/replica-operation-repository-mutation-persistence-methods.js:225`). The gateway delegates those mutations to CDC insert/update helpers (`src/control-plane/control-plane-system-table-gateway-mutation-submission.js:151`-`src/control-plane/control-plane-system-table-gateway-mutation-submission.js:184`, `src/control-plane/control-plane-system-table-gateway-mutation-submission.js:250`-`src/control-plane/control-plane-system-table-gateway-mutation-submission.js:285`), and the CDC helpers explicitly state that the write goes through SQL to the partition leader (`src/cdc/cdc-integration-service-mutation-operations.js:211`-`src/cdc/cdc-integration-service-mutation-operations.js:247`, `src/cdc/cdc-integration-service-mutation-operations.js:357`-`src/cdc/cdc-integration-service-mutation-operations.js:430`).

For an INSERT, the distributed write coordinator resolves the row's target partition from table partitions and the primary key; for a one-partition table like `replica_operations`, `executePlan()` executes one participant (`replica_operations-p1`) and reports the first failed participant from that participant result (`src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:79`-`src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:110`, `src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:141`-`src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:164`, `src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:236`-`src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:315`, `src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:462`-`src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:490`). The table-routing code reads routable partitions/services from the system cache, which CDC keeps synchronized (`src/query/sql-query-engine-table-routing-methods.js:26`-`src/query/sql-query-engine-table-routing-methods.js:35`), and write routing prefers the canonical leader service (`src/query/query-executor-partition-routing-candidates.js:113`-`src/query/query-executor-partition-routing-candidates.js:140`, `src/query/query-executor-partition-routing-candidates.js:171`-`src/query/query-executor-partition-routing-candidates.js:240`).

Therefore `firstFailedParticipant` is the failed partition/candidate selected for `replica_operations-p1`, not evidence that the write fanned out to all removed seed replicas. In every structured `Pending response timeout` failure I found, the failed address is `1ee9936a.../partition/replica_operations-p1-r4`; the first one is at `15:24:46.606Z` (`node-0.log:3704`-`node-0.log:3705`) and the ratings one repeats at `15:29:47.951Z` and `15:30:03.866Z` (`node-0.log:9361`, `node-0.log:9507`-`node-0.log:9510`). There is no matching failed-participant line naming removed seed `r1`/`r3` or node-4 `r5` as the timeout target in this signature.

### What `r4` was doing

`r4` fully activates: node-1 creates it, transitions it to `syncing`, starts its election timer, reaches voter-ready/active, completes replica creation, completes leader handoff, and becomes the liferaft leader (`node-1.log:211`-`node-1.log:214`, `node-1.log:218`-`node-1.log:219`, `node-1.log:273`-`node-1.log:290`). After the storm begins, node-1's `r4` continues to generate CDC evidence for `replica_operations` rows: for operation `a64e92bc...`, node-1 logs a `replica_operations` inserted row at `15:24:30.543Z`, then repeated updated-row CDC fetches through `15:24:37.752Z` (`node-1.log:763`-`node-1.log:802`), before node-0 reports a pending-response timeout for the same operation at `15:24:46.606Z` (`node-0.log:3704`-`node-0.log:3705`). Later, at `15:29:31`-`15:29:34`, node-1 still logs `replica_operations` CDC rows from `r4` while the ratings DDL is starting (`node-1.log:5439`-`node-1.log:5460`, `node-0.log:9215`).

The decisive unhealthy signal is not that `r4` is absent; it is that `r4` is a **durability-unfit leader with an active transaction hold**. At `15:27:12.927Z`, node-1 logs `leader_durability_unfit_transaction_hold`, `role:"leader"`, `successorViable:false`, `heldMs:62036` for `replica_operations-p1-r4` (`node-1.log:2770`). It briefly logs recovered at `15:27:27.716Z` (`node-1.log:3188`), then by `15:28:35.280Z` it again reports one expired active session, defers stuck-transaction heal because it is leader, and marks `r4` durability-unfit with `successorViable:false` (`node-1.log:4256`-`node-1.log:4257`). The stuck-transaction heal deferral continues into the ratings window (`node-1.log:5439`, `node-1.log:5446`). The code explains this exact loop: a leader with an open transaction held beyond `LEADER_DURABILITY_LEGAL_HOLD_MS` becomes unfit (`src/partition/partition-service-durability-fitness.js:219`-`src/partition/partition-service-durability-fitness.js:238`), but demotion runs only when `hasViableLeaderDurabilitySuccessor()` is true (`src/partition/partition-service-durability-fitness.js:77`-`src/partition/partition-service-durability-fitness.js:103`, `src/partition/partition-service-durability-fitness.js:274`-`src/partition/partition-service-durability-fitness.js:321`). The transaction heal itself is deliberately not allowed on a leader/candidate unless the group is solo (`src/partition/partition-service-transaction-base.js:317`-`src/partition/partition-service-transaction-base.js:341`, `src/partition/partition-service-transaction-base.js:388`-`src/partition/partition-service-transaction-base.js:401`).

Classification against the four proposed mechanisms:

- **(a) request never reaches r4 / wrong address:** contradicted. The address is node-1 `r4`, `r4` is active leader, node-1 logs CDC for the affected table, and transport late-response/orphan logs show peers are handling requests (`node-1.log:290`, `node-1.log:763`-`node-1.log:802`, `node-1.log:5365`).
- **(b) r4 receives but never applies / apply loop wedged:** partly supported, but too strong as stated. The logs show `r4` applies at least some `replica_operations` mutations and emits CDC, so it is not a total apply-loop freeze. However the active transaction hold/durability-unfit leader state can stall or delay completion beyond the pending-response window (`node-1.log:2770`, `node-1.log:4256`-`node-1.log:4257`).
- **(c) r4 applies but response is lost/late/misrouted:** supported for the general class by CDC-before-timeout and `No pending service response request` late-response warnings (`node-1.log:763`-`node-1.log:802`, `node-1.log:5365`), but the logs do not carry message ids from `Failed to insert/update system table row` into the remote handler/response path, so I cannot prove a specific row's response was lost rather than just delayed past timeout and then retried.
- **(d) coordinator participant-set/routing stale, with r4 merely first-listed:** contradicted for the main signature. The write has one partition participant and routes writes to the canonical leader candidate; the logs consistently name `r4`, not removed seed replicas or node-4 `r5` (`src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:141`-`src/query/distributed/distributed-write-coordinator.js:164`, `src/query/query-executor-partition-routing-candidates.js:171`-`src/query/query-executor-partition-routing-candidates.js:240`, `node-0.log:3704`, `node-0.log:9361`, `node-0.log:9507`).

## D3. Why the failure persists for 5.5 minutes

The persistence is a feedback failure: retries keep re-presenting ledger writes to the same unhealthy canonical leader, while the mechanisms that could make that leader safe or route around it do not complete.

| Healing path | What should happen | What the logs/code show instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leader durability-fitness demotion | A durability-unfit leader should shed leadership to a viable successor. | The detector fires on `r4` but logs `successorViable:false`, so the code returns without `performTrackedLeaderDemotion()` (`src/partition/partition-service-durability-fitness.js:274`-`src/partition/partition-service-durability-fitness.js:321`). Live: `15:27:12.927Z` and `15:28:35.280Z` both report `successorViable:false` for `r4` (`node-1.log:2770`, `node-1.log:4257`). |

Live: node-1 repeats `Stuck transaction heal deferred...waiting for durability-fitness demotion` with `role:"leader"`, `expiredActiveSessionCount:1` from `15:28:32.802Z` into the ratings window (`node-1.log:4232`-`node-1.log:4257`, `node-1.log:5439`, `node-1.log:5446`). |
| SQL participant retry / rerouting | A failed canonical leader write could widen to recovery candidates. | The query executor has retry states including `widen_to_recovery_candidate` and queues recovery candidates on retryable failures (`src/query/query-executor-shared.js:143`-`src/query/query-executor-shared.js:148`, `src/query/query-executor-partition-delivery.js:660`-`src/query/query-executor-partition-delivery.js:695`, `src/query/query-executor-write-retry-routing.js:486`-`src/query/query-executor-write-retry-routing.js:545`). There is no evidence that retry routing selected a successor that completed the ledger write. |
| Mark bad route unhealthy | Repeated timeouts should stop immediately selecting `r4`. | The temporary-unroutable marker is scoped to no-handler stale-address witnesses, not pending-response timeouts (`src/query/query-executor-partition-candidate-state.js:134`-`src/query/query-executor-partition-candidate-state.js:148`, `src/query/query-executor-temporary-unroutable-addresses.js:11`-`src/query/query-executor-temporary-unroutable-addresses.js:55`). The interlock's own contract serializes disruptive ledger self-moves because every operation persists into this ledger (`src/rebalancer/rebalance-coordinator-ledger-interlock-admission.js:71`-`src/rebalancer/rebalance-coordinator-ledger-interlock-admission.js:121`, `src/rebalancer/rebalance-coordinator-ledger-interlock-admission.js:192`-`src/rebalancer/rebalance-coordinator-ledger-interlock-admission.js:223`). |

## D4. Why `published_active_coverage_incomplete` and `prioritySpreadPending:true` persist

The late convergence trace is explicit that the durable publication has all five active nodes, but the active-gate catch-up fence has no snapshot coverage proof:

```text

  missingPublishedCount=0 expectedNodeCount=5 publishedActiveNodeCount=5
  contractState=degraded contractReason=published_active_coverage_incomplete
  fencePromotionAllowed=false fenceMissingProofReasons=["snapshot_coverage_unavailable"]
  fenceDurablePublicationState=available fenceDurablePublicationMissingCount=0
  fenceSnapshotCoverageState=unavailable fenceSnapshotCoverageMissingCount=5
  prioritySpreadPending=true priorityRecoveryReasonCodes=["priority_partitions_not_spread"]
```

The same state repeats at `15:30:07.119Z` (`node-3.log:2722`). Code matches the log: `published_active_coverage_incomplete` is the reason constant (`src/control-plane/publication-active-gate-handoff-contract-constants.js:28`-`src/control-plane/publication-active-gate-handoff-contract-constants.js:35`), and `buildPublicationActiveGateHandoffContract()` chooses that reason when runtime promotion would otherwise be allowed but the active-gate catch-up fence denies promotion (`src/control-plane/publication-active-gate-handoff-contract.js:276`-`src/control-plane/publication-active-gate-handoff-contract.js:300`). The fence computes snapshot coverage from active-node view/snapshot coverage fields, marks it `unavailable` when no node ids or revision exist, and denies promotion unless the decision state is `PROMOTION_ALLOWED` (`src/control-plane/publication-active-gate-handoff-contract-fence.js:227`-`src/control-plane/publication-active-gate-handoff-contract-fence.js:266`, `src/control-plane/publication-active-gate-handoff-contract-fence.js:390`-`src/control-plane/publication-active-gate-handoff-contract-fence.js:435`).

`prioritySpreadPending:true` is a separate but related signal. The priority-control-plane set includes `control_plane_publications`, `replica_operations`, `sql_transactions`, `sql_transaction_participants`, and `sql_write_operations` (`src/bootstrap/system-partition-classification.js:17`-`src/bootstrap/system-partition-classification.js:23`). The priority-spread decision keeps `priority_partitions_not_spread` when priority spread is still pending or evidence is unavailable (`src/control-plane/publication-recovery-priority-spread.js:200`-`src/control-plane/publication-recovery-priority-spread.js:216`, `src/control-plane/publication-recovery-priority-spread.js:219`-`src/control-plane/publication-recovery-priority-spread.js:244`), and the active-gate snapshot witness returns pending with blocked priority partition ids when unresolved decisions remain (`src/control-plane/priority-recovery-snapshot-active-gate.js:405`-`src/control-plane/priority-recovery-snapshot-active-gate.js:450`).

The live partitions named by `control_plane_replicas_not_spread` are downstream priority partitions, not just the ledger itself: `control_plane_publications-p1`, `sql_transaction_participants-p1`, `sql_write_operations-p1`, and `sql_transactions-p1` all log that reason during the storm (first/last examples: `node-0.log:3212`/`node-3.log:955`, `node-0.log:3243`/`node-3.log:2015`, `node-0.log:3204`/`node-1.log:1431`, `node-0.log:3033`/`node-0.log:3636`). At `15:30:00`, the active rebalancer is still planning `control_plane_publications-p1` work for `sql_transactions-p1` and `sql_transaction_participants-p1`, plus removals of failed `control_plane_publications` replicas, but those moves are skipped due conflicts/budget (`node-3.log:2569`-`node-3.log:2578`). At `15:30:07`, `sql_transaction_participants-p1` removals are still blocked by safety because a replacement is not voter-ready (`node-3.log:2723`-`node-3.log:2727`).

So D4 is not “the publication forgot five nodes”: `missingPublishedCount` is 0. It is “the active-gate handoff cannot prove snapshot coverage while priority partitions remain unsettled,” and the unsettled priority partitions are downstream control-plane partitions whose operation rows are being inserted/updated through the unhealthy `replica_operations-p1` leader.

## D5. Mechanism chain and falsifiers

Bootstrap forms `replica_operations-p1` as `r1/r2/r3` on the seed; spread then succeeds in moving leadership to node-1 `r4`, adding node-4 `r5`, and adding node-2 `r6` while removing `r1` and `r3`. The chain breaks at **[X] = the post-spread canonical ledger leader `r4` remains a surplus, durability-unfit/stuck-transaction leader, and every `replica_operations` row write keeps routing to it without completing a service response before the pending-response timeout**.
